# LinkedIn Moments — Doormat Package Detector

- 2026-07-05: Backpack held up to webcam → detected as "person" at 0.85 confidence. Camera saw an arm/torso holding the bag and confidently ignored the backpack entirely — live proof of why the doormat camera is mounted top-down (no limbs, no faces, no confusion). Initially camera position decision was taken predominantly based on privacy and legal reasons but then we also had another strong reason to go with a vertical top down camera angle.
- 2026-07-05: Phone on carpet → detected as "mouse" at 0.31 confidence. Low-confidence, clearly wrong guess — became the first real data point for deciding what confidence threshold the doormat script should require before trusting a detection enough to notify.
- 2026-07-05: Caught a design flaw in temporal reasoning logic before writing any code. Realized a naive "empty vs not-empty" doormat check would miss a second package if it arrived while the first was still sitting there uncollected — fixed by designing the logic to track object count, not just presence.
- 2026-07-06: Inserted the microSD card wrong-side-up into the adapter during Pi setup and got it stuck — improvised a fix with a cut plastic strip and folded double-sided tape to slide in and pull it free without damaging the pins or the OS. A reminder that hardware mistakes can't be undone like software bugs — you problem-solve with your hands, with real risk of loss.
- 2026-07-06: First successful ping and SSH login into the Raspberry Pi from a laptop over home WiFi, zero networking background going in — confirmed the full chain (flash card, boot, join WiFi, remote login) worked end to end.
- 2026-07-06: Set up passwordless SSH key auth from laptop to Pi and confirmed remote command execution end-to-end — full loop of "control real hardware from code" now working, zero networking background going in.
- 2026-07-09: While collecting training images to fine-tune YOLO on packages, noticed a neighborhood WhatsApp group full of ready-made, realistic package photos — then recognized that data was shared for a different purpose and passed on it, going with own photos/stock/opted-in friends instead. Same instinct that put the camera top-down to begin with (no faces, no bystanders) — both traced back to PII habits picked up working proof-of-delivery systems at Walmart, showing up here without deciding to apply them.

- 2026-07-09: Post idea — "almost trading away the point of the project without noticing." YOLO nano genuinely failed to detect real package photos (not misclassified — nothing fired, at any confidence). Two instant "fixes" came to mind: bigger model, or lower the confidence threshold. Both got pushed back on: a bigger model likely can't run on the actual constrained hardware it needs to live on; a lower threshold can't rescue a detection that never fired — there was no weak signal to surface, just nothing. Both instincts were the same mistake in different clothes — reach for more power the moment something underperforms, without asking what it costs or whether it fixes the real problem. What caught it wasn't ML know-how, it was a plain PM question asked twice: what does this actually cost, and does it even fix what's broken. Deeper point: the whole reason to build small and local (private, free, offline, always-on) is exactly what gets quietly traded away every time "just make it smarter" gets accepted without scrutiny — easy to start a project justifying a constraint, just as easy to abandon it one convenient upgrade at a time, without ever deciding to. Angle: not an edge-vs-cloud tutorial — the post is about catching yourself mid-optimization, twice, and noticing the habit that caught it wasn't technical at all.
- 2026-07-22: The model went from unusable to genuinely trustworthy — first fine-tune topped out at 5.5% confidence on any detection, anywhere (technically "learned" something, practically useless). Second attempt: 93% median confidence, 94.5% of images above 50%. What changed wasn't cleverness, it was scale — consolidating a much larger, messier dataset (9,555 images pulled together from multiple public sources, all under different class names, unified into one) and getting past a solo free-tier GPU quota wall by training on a roommate's Google account instead. Sometimes the fix for "the model doesn't work" isn't a smarter approach, it's just more of the unglamorous stuff (more data, more compute) and knowing who to ask.
- 2026-07-24: First real end-to-end win at the actual doorstep — moved the Pi outside, plugged it in, pointed it at a real package, and the fine-tuned model called it correctly at 87% confidence, no laptop, no lab conditions, just the device doing the one thing it was built to do. Weeks of dataset wrangling, failed training runs, and a borrowed GPU quota, boiled down to one clean, confident detection on a real doorstep.
